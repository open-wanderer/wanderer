## Escalation: The Code Review's Two Named BLOCKERs, Assessed Independently

`38.1-REVIEW.md` reports two BLOCKERs it says are outside the three the phase set out to close. I
verified both directly against the current codebase rather than deferring to the review's prose.
They are **not equivalent** in severity or in relationship to this phase's stated goal, and I am
treating them differently:

### Reviewer's "CR-02" (`deleteLocalTrailRow` missing membership guard) — I concur: this is a real FAILURE of the phase's own goal, not an adjacent defect

Verified directly: `deleteLocalTrailRow` (`local_trail_store.dart:782-806`) has no
`shouldDeleteUploadedRow(entity.savedByUserIds)` check, unlike its sibling
`retireUploadedLocalTrail` a few lines below it in the same file, which already has exactly this
guard. I traced a concrete, **non-exploit, non-stale** reachability path (see the `gaps:` entry
above): account A legitimately deleting their own partially-uploaded capture — a row that account
B has, entirely independently, downloaded into the same `TrailEntity` row because
`TrailEntity.id` is `@Unique(onConflict: replace)` — silently destroys account B's library
membership and orphans `library/<id>/`. This requires no account-switch, no cache staleness, and
no malicious actor: it is the phase's own named overlap scenario, executed by its rightful owner,
on the one destructive path (`deleteUnsynced` → `deleteLocalTrailRow`) that plan 04 did not
extend its guard to (plan 04 only fixed `TrailLibraryNotifier.deleteTrail`, the *other*
destructive path). I classify this as a **BLOCKER**, structured in the `gaps:` frontmatter above
for `/gsd-plan-phase --gaps`.

### Reviewer's "CR-01" (`ownLiveCaptureProvider`/`localTrailProvider` cache staleness across an account switch) — real, but I assess this differently than the reviewer's BLOCKER framing; escalating rather than failing outright

Verified directly: both providers are `@riverpod` auto-dispose families keyed only on `localId`;
`currentAccountId(store)` is resolved inside each `build()`, not as part of the family key; and
neither provider appears in `accountScopedProviders` (`account_scope_invalidation.dart:71-84`),
which is the only list `main.dart:154` invalidates on an auth user-id change. I confirmed this is
architecturally reachable: Flutter's `Navigator` keeps a pushed-under route's widget subtree
mounted (not disposed) while another route is on top, so a screen watching
`localTrailProvider`/`ownLiveCaptureProvider`, then obscured by a pushed route, then revealed by a
pop **after an account switch happened while it was hidden**, will keep rendering its last-built
output because nothing notifies it to rebuild.

Where I diverge from treating this as an equally-severe BLOCKER: I traced what this staleness can
actually *cause*, and the answer is narrower than "a destructive action is scoped by a field read
off a shared cache row" (this phase's literal goal text):

- The **content-leak** consequence (`trail_detail_screen.dart`/`trail_detail_map_screen.dart`
  rendering account A's stale `Trail` to account B) is real and is a legitimate privacy defect,
  but it is not itself a *destructive action* — nothing is deleted or overwritten by viewing
  stale content. It sits outside this phase's stated goal and outside the literal wording of
  DL-01/DL-07.
- The **stale Delete-gate** consequence is real, but I confirmed the actual destructive call —
  `TrailSync.deleteUnsynced` — re-reads `currentAccountId(store)` **fresh**, at the point of the
  transaction, independent of the (possibly stale) UI gate that decided whether to *offer* Delete.
  A stale-`true` gate showing account B a Delete item they should not see routes into a store call
  that is correctly owner-scoped and will refuse the deletion (no row matches `owner: B`). The
  reviewer's own review text reaches the same conclusion: "this degrades to a misleading error
  toast rather than data loss." I agree with that specific technical claim.

Given the phase's goal is explicitly about *destructive actions*, and the reachable consequence of
this specific gap is (a) a privacy/content-freshness issue outside that goal's literal scope, and
(b) a misleading error toast rather than actual cross-account destruction, I am **not** classifying
this as a BLOCKER on the same footing as the `deleteLocalTrailRow` gap. I am flagging it as a
**WARNING requiring a human decision**, because:

- It is real, it is trivially fixable (add both providers to `accountScopedProviders`, per the
  reviewer's own suggested fix), and the code's own doc comments in `trail_dropdown.dart` assert an
  invariant ("an account-scoped value must be read fresh at the point of use, never from a
  long-lived cache") that this specific pair of providers does not actually enforce mechanically.
- Whether that inconsistency is worth blocking this phase's closure, versus scheduling a fast
  follow-up alongside the `deleteLocalTrailRow` gap closure, is a judgment call about severity and
  timing that I am surfacing rather than deciding unilaterally.

**This looks like it may be intentional to defer, but I am not asserting that.** If the developer
agrees this is out of the phase's literal scope, add an override:

    accepted_by: "{your name}"
