<objective>
Close UAT gap 5 (major): opening a trail whose server record no longer exists traps the hiker
on a chromeless spinner for ~38 seconds before showing a raw exception.

`TrailNotifier.build` 404s and its ObjectBox fallback (`trail_provider.dart:70-96`) is gated on
`savedByUserIds.containsElement(userId)`, which a local capture never gains and — per D-10 —
must never gain. So line 96 `rethrow`s the `DioException` into Riverpod 3's `defaultRetry`
(`provider_container.dart:831-845`): 10 attempts at 200/400/800/1600/3200/6400×5, and because
`DioException` is neither an `Error` nor a `ProviderException`, every one of them fires. Each
pending retry emits `AsyncLoading(retrying: true)`, which `AsyncValue.when`'s default
`skipLoadingOnReload: false` renders as `loading()` — `trail_detail_screen.dart:196-199`, a
full-screen `CircularProgressIndicator` with no AppBar, no back button and no cancel. Only after
the tenth does `WandererError` appear, showing `err.toString()`: a DioException dump.

A 404 is a permanent condition. Retrying it ten times is a self-inflicted denial of service.

This defect is independent of the orphan that exposed it: it fires for ANY trail deleted
elsewhere — from the web UI, from another device, by an admin. 36-09 gave `trailFilterProvider`
a bounded retry policy and does not touch `trailProvider`, so this survives every other plan in
the phase.

Purpose: REC-02 puts the hiker's trails in a list they are expected to tap. SYNC-05 keeps a
uploaded trail's identity in that list. Neither promise survives a row that opens onto a
38-second blank spinner. The blast radius genuinely exceeds both requirements — it is charged
to them because that is where the gap was found, not because it is bounded by them.

Output: a terminal-failure classification with a bounded retry budget wired onto
`trailProvider`, and a detail screen that always has a back button and says plainly when a
trail is gone.
</objective>
